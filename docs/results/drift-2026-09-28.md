# Drift alarms on τ²-bench agents

A flow is learned from one agent, one prompt, one harness and one set of tools, and any of them can change. `stretto drift` scores the sessions a flow served, in the order they ran, for three things: the surprise of the agent's steps under the flow, how often the flow's likeliest option was not the agent's step, and the share of the agent's steps after a tool training never saw it call. Bayesian online change-point detection (Adams and MacKay, 2007) watches the three together, and the alarm sounds when a change in the last ten sessions is more likely than not ([issue #18](https://github.com/alexnodeland/stretto/issues/18)).

This round checks the alarm on real agents spliced together: one agent's sessions, then another's, and one agent's sessions alone in random orders. It also checks `learn --half-life`, which weighs recent sessions more when a flow is learned again.

## Findings

- **One agent's own sessions raised 5 false alarms in 160 random orders.** Each run was an agent's 160 retail or 80 airline test episodes, shuffled.
  - Requiring a new run of two sessions, the alarm sounded in 18 of the 160.
  - Most of those were two trials of one task side by side, both calling a tool the flow never saw. The alarm now needs three sessions of a new run.
- **Where a new agent fits the flow worse, the alarm finds it within about eight sessions.** On the three splices where the new agent's surprise rose by 0.3 nats or more, it sounded in 26 of 60 runs, a median of 7.5 sessions after the splice.
  - It found Claude 3.7 Sonnet giving way to GPT-4.1-mini (0.69 → 1.15 nats) in 16 of 20.
  - It found Claude 3.7 giving way to o4-mini (0.69 → 1.00) in 8 of 20, and GPT-4.1 giving way to GPT-4.1-mini (0.72 → 1.05) in 2 of 20.
- **It finds a new agent that calls tools the flow never saw, even at the same surprise.** In airline, the other three agents call `calculate` and `list_all_airports`, which o4-mini never called in training. Under o4-mini's flow, the alarm found them in 32 of 60 runs, a median of 10 sessions in, while surprise moved by less than 0.05 nats.
- **Where the new agent fits about as well, it stays quiet.** On the other 18 splices, where surprise moved by less than 0.3 nats and no new tool appeared, it sounded in 2 of 360 runs. Claude 3.7 giving way to GPT-4.1, for one, moved the retail flow's surprise from 0.69 to 0.79 nats. The alarm is for a flow that no longer fits, and that one still does.
- **A new harness moved the fit not at all.** Claude 3.7's retail flow, learned in τ²-bench's harness, was served GLM-5.3 in Claude Code: [the paired run's](paired-2026-09-25.md) 40 sessions. Their surprise was 0.694 nats, against 0.692 on Claude 3.7's own test episodes, and the alarm stayed quiet. Claude Code runs reads in parallel, but in the same order, so the habit's next-step predictions still fit.
- **Forgetting follows a new agent without discarding the old.** The retail flow was relearned from Claude 3.7's training episodes, then GPT-4.1-mini's, with no forgetting and with a half-life. On GPT-4.1-mini's test episodes, surprise was:
  - 0.83 nats with no forgetting;
  - 0.77 with a half-life of 100 episodes;
  - 0.87 with a flow learned from GPT-4.1-mini's episodes alone.

  The old agent's episodes still help, weighed less. A half-life of 25 kept too few episodes (0.84).

## How the alarm decides

Each session gives three scores under the flow, decided as `stretto audit` decides, by the habit alone by default:

- **surprise:** the mean negative log-probability of the agent's steps, in nats;
- **disagreement:** the share of the flow's decisions where its likeliest option was not the agent's step;
- **unknown:** the share of the agent's steps after a tool training never saw it call. The flow makes no decision there, so surprise does not see these steps.

Within a run of sessions, each score is Gaussian, with a mean and a variance of its own under a weak normal-gamma prior. The prior is centred on the score's median, with the spread of its session-to-session steps and at least 0.05. A session is then predicted by a Student-t, except that any session may be an outlier drawn from the prior, with a chance of 5%. A session counts toward a run as far as it belongs to it, so one odd session leaves the run as it was.

After each session, the posterior over how long the current run has lasted gives the probability that the agent changed recently. That means a new run that began within the last ten sessions (`--window`), after at least ten sessions, and has lasted at least three. The alarm sounds when that probability reaches 0.5 (`--threshold`), with a prior chance of a change of 1% per session (`--hazard`).

## Setup

- **Agents:** the four with results in both domains in τ²-bench's repository: Claude 3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1-mini and o4-mini, each with four trials of every task.
- **Flows:** for each agent and domain, a flow of the habit alone, learned from the agent's episodes on the training tasks (`stretto learn --habit-only --results`).
- **Runs:** on the test tasks, 160 episodes per agent in retail and 80 in airline, in 20 random orders.
  - An agent alone: its test episodes, shuffled.
  - A splice: its test episodes, then another agent's, each shuffled.
- **Scoring:**
  - An alarm that sounds before the splice is a false one.
  - The first alarm to sound within two windows of the splice, with its change no earlier than two sessions before it, finds the splice. Its delay is the sessions from the splice to the alarm.

## What it does not show

- **Small changes pass unseen.** A shift of 0.1 to 0.3 nats in a session's mean surprise is about a third of a session's spread. It would take more sessions than the window allows to tell it from chance. Such a flow still fits nearly as well, and `stretto audit` measures the fit over any span.
- **The splices are benchmark agents, not a deployment.** A deployment's own change, such as a new model version behind the same name, a prompt change or a tool update, was not staged here. [The unit and CLI tests](../../crates/stretto-report/src/drift.rs) splice an agent that reads the customer's rewards with a tool training never saw. The alarm sounds three sessions after the splice, and names the tool and the site where the flow's surprise rose most.
- **A change in the traffic is a change too.** Requests of one kind in a burst can look like a new agent. Three alike in a row are rare among 40 tasks in random order; real traffic may run longer.

## Reproduce

```bash
cargo build -p stretto-report
python3 scripts/drift_splices.py --tau2 PATH/TO/tau2-bench --stretto target/debug/stretto --orders 20 \
    --out docs/results/drift-2026-09-28.json
```

[drift-2026-09-28.json](drift-2026-09-28.json) holds every run's row.

The harness splice and forgetting:

```bash
T=PATH/TO/tau2-bench; R=$T/data/tau2/results/final
C=$R/claude-3-7-sonnet-20250219_retail_default_gpt-4.1-2025-04-14_4trials.json
M=$R/gpt-4.1-mini-2025-04-14_retail_base_gpt-4.1-2025-04-14_4trials.json
stretto learn --domain retail --habit-only --tau2 $T --results $C --out claude.flow.json
tar -xzf docs/results/paired-2026-09-25-episodes.tar.gz -C /tmp
mkdir -p /tmp/cc-retail && cp /tmp/paired-2026-09-25-episodes/retail/baseline/*/log/[0-9]*[0-9].jsonl /tmp/cc-retail/
stretto drift --flow claude.flow.json --results $C --tau2 $T --sessions /tmp/cc-retail
for h in 25 100; do
  stretto learn --domain retail --habit-only --tau2 $T --results $C --results $M --half-life $h --out h$h.flow.json
  stretto drift --flow h$h.flow.json --results $M --tau2 $T --json h$h.json
done
```
