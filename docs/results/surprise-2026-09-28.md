# Handing back on surprise, replayed on τ²-bench agents

`stretto learn --surprise Q` gives a flow a surprise gate ([issue #17](https://github.com/alexnodeland/stretto/issues/17), RFC-001 §3.6). After each call where the flow decides, the gate scores the agent's next step by its surprise under the habit, −ln p. Once the agent's steps average more than a threshold over a window of steps in a row, the flow hands back for the rest of the session. The threshold is the Q-quantile of the most surprising window in each training session, each scored by a habit that did not learn from its task ([audit and review](https://stretto.alexnodeland.com/guide/concepts/audit-and-review#hand-back-when-a-session-surprises-the-flow)). This round replays the gate on two agents' test episodes in two domains, at five quantiles over four windows, and asks what it trades: detours avoided against LLM turns saved lost.

## Findings

- **At the quantiles a deployment would choose, the gate changes almost nothing.** Learned at 0.8 to 0.99, over windows of 1 to 5 steps, it tripped in 0 to 18 of 160 retail sessions and 0 to 15 of 80 airline ones. In 62 of those 64 runs, turns saved and detours each moved by at most one. The other two were in GLM-5's retail, at 0.8:
  - over one step, the gate cost 9 saved turns to avoid 3 detours;
  - over two, it avoided 2 detours for nothing.
- **It trips too late.** Where it tripped, the flow had already made most of its lookups: at the 0.5 quantile over one or two steps, 90% to 100% of them, depending on the agent and domain. Detours come in the burst of reads after the customer is found, and the steps that surprise the flow come after that, as [the detours round](detours-2026-09-26.md) found for a hand-back told of each detour at once.
- **Only an aggressive gate avoided detours, and it paid in one place at a time.** The 0.5 quantile is one that half of the training sessions trip. At it, the gate tripped in up to 93 of 160 retail sessions and 40 of 80 airline ones:
  - in GLM-5's retail, over two steps, it avoided 5 of 41 detours and lost no turn; over one step, it lost 9 turns for 8 detours;
  - in GLM-5's airline, over one step, it avoided 4 of 5 detours and lost no turn;
  - in Claude 3.7 Sonnet's airline, over one step, it lost 3 turns for 6 of 57 detours;
  - in Claude 3.7's retail, it avoided none.
- **No setting paid in more than one of the four.** Priced at the paper's costs, a saved turn is worth about 2.4 detours in retail and 7 in airline. The best setting, two steps at 0.5, gained 2.1 turns' worth in GLM-5's retail (0.7% of its 315 saved turns), none in its airline, and lost in both of Claude 3.7's domains.

## What it means

The default stays off: a flow has a gate only when `learn --surprise` gives it one. On these agents, a flow's work in a session is done before the session has shown whether it is unusual, so there is nothing left for the gate to stop.

Where sessions go off script later than the flow's lookups, the gate is built and cheap to try. The proxy serves a learned gate with `--flow-surprise`, or turns it off with `--flow-surprise off`, and `stretto promote` and `stretto stage` score a gated flow as it will be served. [Promotion](promotion-2026-09-25.md) remains what cuts detours: it removes the sites where the flow's lookups go unused, whenever in the session they come.

## Setup

- **Agents and domains:** GLM-5 and Claude 3.7 Sonnet, retail and airline, from their published τ²-bench results (GLM-5's four trials with GPT-5.2 as the customer, Claude 3.7's with GPT-4.1).
- **The flows** are learned from each agent's episodes on the training tasks, with the habit alone (`stretto learn --habit-only --results`), as a deployment's first flow is. One has no gate. The others have a gate learned at each quantile, 0.99, 0.95, 0.9, 0.8 and 0.5, over windows of 1, 2, 3 and 5 of the agent's steps (`--surprise Q --surprise-window N`).
- **Serving:** as `stretto init` serves a learned flow, with `reach` at 0.3.
- **The replay** is `pilot/check_flow.py`'s exact replay of each agent's test episodes, every trial: 160 retail and 80 airline episodes per agent.
  - The recorded conversation is replayed through τ²-bench's tools, and the flow decides after each of the agent's calls, as `stretto serve` answers the harness.
  - A recorded LLM turn is saved when the flow's lookups answered all of its calls, and a lookup that answered none is a detour.
  - The flow's lookups carry the proxy's ids (`stretto-1`, `stretto-2`, …), so the gate does not count them as the agent's steps.
  - A session trips the gate when the flow handed back in it because the session surprised it (`--explore 0` records each answer).
- **Pricing:** at the paper's costs, one saved turn is worth about 2.4 detours in retail and about 7 in airline ([benchmarks](benchmarks-2026-09-27.md)). A gate pays when the detours it avoids, so priced, outweigh the turns it loses.

## What it does not show

- **A replay assumes the agent acts as it did.** After a hand-back the agent makes the calls the flow would have made, as recorded. Live, an agent that had been handed the flow's results might have gone on differently.
- **Two benchmark agents on their own tasks.** The test tasks differ from the training tasks, but the agent is the one the flow learned from. A session unlike the training ones by more, such as another agent's, is what the gate is for, and [the drift alarm](drift-2026-09-28.md) is the check across sessions.
- **One kind of flow.** The habit alone, served with `reach`. A flow with an arbiter scores the agent's steps by its habit too, but it makes other lookups, and so the gate sees other steps.
