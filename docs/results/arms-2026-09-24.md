# Arms C and D0: what arbitration adds, and what naming a flow would

RFC-001 planned `plan_*` macro-tools that the LLM calls by name (arms C and D). It then settled first on arm D0, a flow that runs behind the agent's own calls. That left two questions:

- How much of D0's saving comes from *arbitration*, where the System-One model's answers are weighed against the habit? How much would the habit get alone, and how much would a flow get if it went on only where training shows no branch (arm C)?
- How much could *naming* a flow add over D0?

Both are answered here without an LLM. The only spend was new Jev questions that D0's replays asked: 593 answers, $0.078.

## What arbitration adds

[`pilot/check_flow.py`](../../pilot/check_flow.py) replays recorded episodes through τ²-bench's tools, over MCP, with a flow behind them (see [the pilot's replay check](../../pilot/README.md#check-the-flow-without-an-llm)):

- A recorded turn whose calls the flow had already made is a turn saved.
- A flow lookup the agent never made is a detour. A detour costs tokens, not actions: flows only read.

All four flows are compiled from the 2025 baselines' training tasks, goal free, and take the likeliest lookup when its probability, times the chance that its bound arguments are the agent's, clears the threshold:

- **D0.** The arbiter decides: the habit, Jev's answers and the predicates, combined. Threshold 0.3. The live pilots ran this flow.
- **Habit alone.** The habit's prediction in place of the arbiter's, at the same threshold. The flow never asks Jev (`--flow-decider habit`).
- **Arm C.** The habit alone at 0.9.
- **Strict arm C.** The habit alone at 0.99. The flow goes on only where training shows no branch, and hands every branch back, as TraceCompiler does.

**GLM-5**, a transfer target, on its recorded test episodes, all four trials. The habit never trained on GLM-5 or on these tasks. Retail has 160 episodes and 1,384 LLM turns; airline has 80 and 630.

| Flow | Retail: turns saved | Retail: lookups the agent also made | Retail: episodes with a detour | Airline: turns saved | Airline: lookups the agent also made | Airline: episodes with a detour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D0: arbiter, 0.3 | 294 (21.2%) | 598 of 656 (91%) | 27 | 41 (6.5%) | 184 of 210 (88%) | 8 |
| Habit alone, 0.3 | 311 (22.5%) | 591 of 649 (91%) | 27 | 55 (8.7%) | 200 of 250 (80%) | 20 |
| Arm C: habit, 0.9 | 24 (1.7%) | 24 of 24 | 0 | 0 | – | 0 |
| Strict arm C: habit, 0.99 | 0 | – | 0 | 0 | – | 0 |

D0's retail row reproduces the replay check the live pilot was built on (294 turns, 21.2%). Comparing the habit alone with D0, paired by episode and bootstrapped over tasks (4,000 draws):

- **Retail:** the habit alone saves 1.2 points more (95% interval −0.5 to +3.0). Detours are the same: 58 each. 111 of 160 episodes save exactly as many turns.
- **Airline:** the habit alone saves 2.2 points more (+0.6 to +4.2), and makes twice the detours: 50 against 26.

Airline saves less here than live (17.3% with GLM-5.3 in Claude Code). GLM-5 in τ²-bench's harness makes several calls per turn, and a turn is saved only when the flow made all of them (amendment 4, finding 3).

**Claude 3.7 Sonnet**, one of the four source agents, on its test episodes, all four trials. The arbiter of each task's fold never saw the task. Retail has 160 episodes and 2,163 turns; airline has 80 and 1,081. Only D0 and the habit alone were replayed:

| Flow | Retail: turns saved | Retail: lookups the agent also made | Retail: episodes with a detour | Airline: turns saved | Airline: lookups the agent also made | Airline: episodes with a detour |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| D0: arbiter, 0.3 | 572 (26.4%) | 536 of 609 (88%) | 33 | 180 (16.7%) | 148 of 177 (84%) | 15 |
| Habit alone, 0.3 | 597 (27.6%) | 561 of 662 (85%) | 48 | 208 (19.2%) | 176 of 271 (65%) | 28 |

The habit alone saves 1.2 points more in retail (−0.6 to +2.9) and 2.6 more in airline (+1.1 to +4.5). Its detours: 101 against 73 in retail, 95 against 29 in airline.

Three findings:

1. **Hand every branch back, and a flow saves almost nothing.** At 0.9 the habit alone made 24 lookups in retail, which saved 1.7% of turns, and none in airline. At 0.99 it made none. Branches are everywhere. Flows pay because they act on a probability, and a wrong read costs only a detour.
2. **Arbitration buys precision, not savings.** For both agents, the habit alone saves as many turns as D0 or slightly more: 1.2 points in retail and 2.2–2.6 in airline. The arbiter's advantage is detours. In airline it makes half (GLM-5) to a third (Claude 3.7 Sonnet) as many. In retail it makes as many (GLM-5) or 28% fewer (Sonnet). Jev's weighed answers make the flow stop where the habit would guess.
3. **So a flow does not need a System-One model to save turns.** `stretto-proxy --flow-decider habit` serves the habit alone: no key, no cost or latency per question, and more detours, mostly in airline. This leaves untested the other roles RFC-001 gives Jev: matching descriptions to records, classifying stated reasons and judging tool outputs. It also leaves untested a deployment with too few traces for a strong habit, where a zero-shot model may matter more. The habit here learned from four agents on 74 training tasks.

## What naming a flow would add

A named macro-tool (`plan_*`) could make one kind of lookup that a flow behind the tools cannot: one that needs a value from the conversation, such as a city the customer named or a date the agent works out. It could do so only if the LLM passed that value in the call. Phase 0 now splits the LLM turns that only look something up and follow another tool call, with no message in between, by where their arguments came from. The table covers all episodes and gives each as a share of all LLM turns:

| Agent model | Retail: from earlier outputs | Retail: from the conversation | Airline: from earlier outputs | Airline: from the conversation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| claude-3-7-sonnet | 31.6% | 0.7% | 30.4% | 3.0% |
| gpt-4.1 | 19.1% | 0.0% | 8.9% | 1.7% |
| gpt-4.1-mini | 11.3% | 0.8% | 5.7% | 0.9% |
| o4-mini | 20.8% | 0.9% | 19.5% | 3.7% |
| glm-5 | 26.0% | 1.6% | 13.7% | 2.9% |
| qwen3.5 | 38.3% | 1.7% | 37.4% | 7.3% |
| qwen3-max | 34.7% | 0.5% | 33.6% | 3.3% |
| gpt-5.2 | 23.7% | 1.5% | 17.3% | 3.3% |
| gpt-5.2-none | 25.6% | 1.4% | 14.7% | 2.8% |
| claude-opus-4.5 | 25.6% | 1.1% | 12.4% | 2.8% |
| claude-sonnet-4.5 | 26.3% | 1.4% | 28.9% | 5.4% |
| gemini-3-pro | 31.5% | 1.6% | 26.7% | 5.4% |
| gemini-3-flash | 33.8% | 1.9% | 35.6% | 7.9% |
| 2025 baselines, pooled | 21.0% | 0.6% | 17.0% | 2.3% |

Naming could add at most 0.0–1.9% of LLM turns in retail and 0.9–7.9% in airline. For GLM-5 that is 1.6% and 2.9%. These are upper bounds. They assume the agent adopts the tool and passes the right values before it has seen the first results. In airline, 92% of these lookups are flight searches, whose dates the agent works out (60% direct, 32% one-stop, over all thirteen agents).

So `plan_*` is not built, and no live run tested it. If naming is ever worth building, it is for airline agents that search many dates, such as Gemini 3 Flash and Qwen3.5.

## Reproduce

[The CLI reference](../cli.md) lists every option.

The replays need the pilot's environment ([pilot/README.md](../../pilot/README.md)) and a replay cache holding the goal-free v2 answers (the v2 bundle and its goal-free supplement). D0's replays also asked new questions, which are in [the next round's answer bundle](answers-2026-09-24-arms-sweep-confirm.md). With all three imported, `--flow-oracle replay` in place of `jev` replays D0 without a key:

```sh
cd pilot
for flow in "arbiter 0.3 jev" "habit 0.3 replay" "habit 0.9 replay" "habit 0.99 replay"; do
  set -- $flow
  python check_flow.py --domain retail --trials 0 1 2 3 \
    --results ../.data/tau2-targets/glm-5_enabled_retail_gpt-5.2_4trials.json \
    --oracle-cache ../.oracle-cache --flow-decider $1 --flow-threshold $2 --flow-oracle $3 \
    --out runs/arms/retail-$1-$2
done
```

Airline is the same with `--domain airline` and GLM-5's airline results. For Claude 3.7 Sonnet, pass τ²-bench's own results file (`../../tau2-bench/data/tau2/results/final/claude-3-7-sonnet-20250219_<domain>_default_gpt-4.1-2025-04-14_4trials.json`). The naming table is the *Lookups inside runs* section of `stretto phase0 --tau2 ../tau2-bench $(scripts/fetch-leaderboard.sh all | sed 's/^/--target /')`. Per-episode rows for every replay are in [arms-2026-09-24.json](arms-2026-09-24.json).
