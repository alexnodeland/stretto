# Telecom, and an arbiter fitted on retail and airline together

τ²-bench's third domain is telecom: technical support for a phone line, where the customer runs tools on their own phone (checking the status bar, toggling airplane mode, rebooting) while the agent works on the account. Every result so far is retail or airline. [The shipped arbiters](arbiter-transfer-2026-09-24.md) each did in the other domain what that domain's own arbiter did. This round asks whether that holds in a domain unlike both, and whether an arbiter fitted on retail and airline together does better. Everything is offline, on τ²-bench's published trajectories of four agents (Claude 3.7 Sonnet, GPT-4.1, GPT-4.1 mini and o4-mini; 456 episodes each). Jev answered 4,713 questions for $0.48.

## Findings

- **Telecom compresses like the others.** Macro-tool headroom is 30% of LLM turns, and 26.7% of turns are lookups whose arguments appeared in an earlier result, so a read-only flow can bind them. The habit's top guess is the agent's next step 69% of the time, as in retail and airline.
- **Jev is weaker here.** Weighed with the habit, its answers agree with the agent's next step 67.9% of the time (80.5% in retail, 78.4% in airline), and its calibration error is 0.16 (0.07 in retail, 0.10 in airline). The questions' wording and the three predicates speak of orders, items and flights, never of phones or lines.
- **Offline, a telecom flow saves 15–26% of LLM turns** with its own arbiter and the lookup-first rule, with detours in 69–93% of episodes.
- **The shipped arbiters do not carry to telecom.** At telecom's 6,343 held-out decisions, telecom's own arbiter made the agent's next lookup 2,747 times. The retail arbiter made it 1,599 times, the airline arbiter 1,828, and an arbiter fitted on both domains' 6,555 decisions 1,638. Fitted where handing back was common and Jev more reliable, they hand back too often here. The two-domain arbiter falls between the two it was fitted from, so `data/arbiters/` does not ship it.
- **The habit alone is the safer start in a new domain.** It made 2,410 of those lookups, 88% of what telecom's own arbiter made, with fewer detours (1,572 against 1,800). A deployment in a domain unlike retail and airline should start on the habit alone, as the cold start found, and fit its own arbiter as its sessions accumulate.

## Phase 0 on telecom

74 training tasks, 40 held-out tasks. 13 of the agent's tools: 6 read, 6 write, 1 other (the transfer).

| Agent model | Success | LLM turns per episode | Tool calls per episode | Macro-tool headroom | Lookups with arguments from earlier outputs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude 3.7 Sonnet | 49% | 17.6 | 6.8 | 20% | 17.4% |
| GPT-4.1 | 34% | 17.1 | 6.7 | 21% | 18.0% |
| GPT-4.1 mini | 44% | 29.1 | 18.2 | 42% | 40.8% |
| o4-mini | 42% | 17.7 | 8.9 | 27% | 21.5% |
| **All** | | | | **30%** | **26.7%** |

The habit (k = 2) puts its top probability on the agent's next step 69% of the time over all four agents (78% for Claude 3.7 Sonnet and GPT-4.1, 64% for GPT-4.1 mini). At a confidence of 0.8 it could take half the decisions and would agree with the agent at 85% of them.

**The customer's phone.** Customers made 12.0 tool calls per episode on their own phones, more than the agents' 10.2. `stretto-trace` keeps each as a customer turn and drops its result: a flow serves the agent, and it cannot call the customer's tools. What the customer reads off the phone reaches the flow only when they say it. So a telecom flow sees less of the state than a retail one, one reason its System-One questions are harder.

## Phase 0b: the System-One questions

The v2 questions, goal free, with the three predicates, at every held-out decision right after a tool returned: 6,520 decisions, 4,713 distinct questions, all answered by jev-1.13.0.

| Agent model | Decisions | One question | Split | Weighed with the habit | Calibration error |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Claude 3.7 Sonnet | 1,172 | 70.3% | 67.4% | 82.1% | 0.074 |
| GPT-4.1 | 1,214 | 52.8% | 55.8% | 70.3% | 0.219 |
| GPT-4.1 mini | 2,675 | 43.1% | 32.4% | 60.7% | 0.232 |
| o4-mini | 1,282 | 66.6% | 68.5% | 67.6% | 0.066 |
| **All** | 6,343 | 54.8% | 50.6% | 67.9% | 0.158 |

The fitted arbiter leans on the habit (weight 0.63) and away from handing back (−0.86) and from the "only the customer can give it" predicate (−0.94). Its record of Jev at telecom's sites is 50% agreement, against 75% in retail.

Projected with the lookup-first rule at 0.3, a flow saves 17.4% of turns for Claude 3.7 Sonnet, 19.0% for GPT-4.1, 25.9% for GPT-4.1 mini and 15.4% for o4-mini, with a detour in 69%, 89%, 93% and 83% of episodes. The full report is [telecom-2026-09-25-report.md](telecom-2026-09-25-report.md).

## Which arbiter, in telecom

`stretto fit-arbiter` fits one arbiter on the decision logs of several compiles (`compile --oracle-log`). Fitted on retail's log alone, or airline's, it gives back the shipped arbiters exactly; fitted on both, it has 6,555 held-out decisions and 26 sites. At a site it never saw, which is every telecom site, an arbiter weighs Jev's pick by its record over all the sites it was fitted on.

At telecom's 6,343 held-out decisions, each way of deciding takes the likeliest lookup when its probability reaches the threshold ([`scripts/compare_arbiters.py`](../../scripts/compare_arbiters.py)). The agent made a lookup at 3,677 of them.

| Deciding with | Lookups | The agent's own lookup | Detours | The flow's action was the agent's |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Telecom's own arbiter (each decision judged by a fold that never saw its task) | 4,547 | 2,747 (74.7%) | 1,800 | 64.2% |
| The habit alone | 3,982 | 2,410 (65.5%) | 1,572 | 61.3% |
| The airline arbiter | 3,071 | 1,828 (49.7%) | 1,243 | 54.2% |
| The retail and airline arbiter | 2,589 | 1,638 (44.5%) | 951 | 54.3% |
| The retail arbiter | 2,489 | 1,599 (43.5%) | 890 | 54.3% |

At a threshold of 0.5 the order is the same. The binding's chance is left out, which can only add lookups, and these are decisions, not replayed episodes. Replayed in retail and airline, the same comparison put every arbiter within about a point of the domain's own ([the transfer](arbiter-transfer-2026-09-24.md)); at retail's held-out decisions, this script puts the airline and two-domain arbiters within one point of retail's own too.

## What changes

- `stretto phase0 --domain telecom` works as it is. The default stays retail and airline, so every published reproduction runs unchanged.
- `stretto fit-arbiter` fits an arbiter on several compiles' decision logs, and `scripts/fetch-leaderboard.sh -t` also fetches each leaderboard model's telecom run.
- `data/arbiters/` keeps its two arbiters, and says they carry between retail and airline, not to a domain unlike both.

## Reproduce

[The CLI reference](../cli.md) lists every option.

Import every published answer bundle, including [this round's](answers-2026-09-25-telecom.md), into `.oracle-cache`. Then, with no key:

```sh
stretto phase0 --tau2 ../tau2-bench --domain telecom --oracle replay --oracle-cache .oracle-cache \
  --questions v2 --no-intent --predicates data/predicates-v2.json --out telecom-report.md
for d in retail airline telecom; do
  stretto compile --tau2 ../tau2-bench --domain $d --oracle replay --oracle-cache .oracle-cache \
    --questions v2 --predicates data/predicates-v2.json --oracle-log log.jsonl --out $d.flow.json
done
stretto fit-arbiter --log log-retail.jsonl --log log-airline.jsonl --predicates data/predicates-v2.json \
  --domain retail+airline --out retail+airline.json
python3 scripts/compare_arbiters.py log-telecom.jsonl telecom.flow.json \
  data/arbiters/retail.json data/arbiters/airline.json retail+airline.json
```

The per-decider numbers at both thresholds are in [telecom-2026-09-25.json](telecom-2026-09-25.json).
