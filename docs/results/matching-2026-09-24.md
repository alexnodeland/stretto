# Matching descriptions to records

RFC-001 lists matching what a customer describes to a record as a decision a System-One model could take from the LLM: "the blue one", "my trip to Denver", "refund it to PayPal". This run tests it on the writes where that matters. Jev picks the record from what the customer said, and both its pick and the agent's are scored against the task's expected actions.

## Method

`stretto match` walks τ²-bench's published trajectories: the four 2025 agents, all tasks, four trials each. At each write that picks records out of earlier tool results, it asks Jev which one the customer means:

- **Items of the order**: the items to return, exchange or modify, among the order's items. Several may be meant, so there is one yes/no question per item.
- **New variant**: what an exchanged or modified item becomes, among its product's variants.
- **Payment method**: which of the customer's payment methods, with the order's or reservation's earlier payments, for "the original one".
- **Reservation** (airline): which reservation to cancel or change, among those the agent looked up.

A choice is asked only when there are at least two candidates, and when the task expects the same write on the same order or reservation, so there is a right answer. Jev sees:

- the customer's messages, the last 12, each up to 600 characters;
- the write about to be made, with the order or reservation it acts on;
- the candidates' records.

It never sees the agent's pick, nor the agent's messages, which would give the pick away. The answer is right when it is the task's: the same set of items, variant or payment method, or any reservation the task expects that write on.

## Results

3,978 choices, 3,708 distinct questions, $0.29.

| | Choices | Agent right | Jev right | Jev differs from the agent | … where the agent was wrong | Agent errors Jev gets right |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retail: items of the order | 1,403 | 93.5% | 81.5% | 227 | 37 (16%) | 21 of 91 |
| Retail: new variant | 1,302 | 91.0% | 83.5% | 143 | 27 (19%) | 18 of 117 |
| Retail: payment method | 506 | 83.0% | 80.6% | 26 | 7 (27%) | 7 of 86 |
| Airline: payment method | 295 | 82.7% | 78.6% | 30 | 12 (40%) | 6 of 51 |
| Airline: reservation | 472 | 87.9% | 79.9% | 185 | 31 (17%) | 11 of 57 |
| All | 3,978 | 89.9% | 81.6% | 611 | 114 (19%) | 63 of 402 |

- **Jev matches records less well than the agents do**, in every kind of choice: 82% against 90% overall.
- **As a check on writes, it is mostly noise.** Where its pick differs from the agent's, the agent was wrong 19% of the time. Flagging those would catch 63 of the agents' 402 wrong picks.
- **Confidence does not rescue it.** Counting only picks Jev makes with probability 0.9 or more, it flags 81 writes. The agent was wrong on 32 of them, and Jev was right on 25.
- **Where it helps:**
  - The customer asked for a refund to PayPal, and the agent used the credit card.
  - An agent exchanged one item per call where the task exchanges both at once. τ²-bench allows one exchange per order, so the second call fails.
- **Where it fails:** most often it leaves out items the customer asked for. Of its 260 wrong sets of items, 152 are missing items, and 107 of those pick none at all. After "I'd like to return all of the items in that order", it gave each item 0.42 to 0.46. Another 96 add items the task does not return.
- **Two design choices do not explain the gap.** One is leaving the item being replaced out of the variants. The other is counting an item as picked at 0.3 instead of 0.5. Each moves Jev's accuracy by under two points.

## What this means

- **From the customer's words alone, a System-One model matches records worse than the agent does.** The agent asked the questions and read the answers. The customer's side alone is often ambiguous: "Yes, the second one", or items named once to keep and once to return.
- **It is not a useful check on writes, as asked here.** Showing the agent's messages would let the model copy the agent's pick, so there is no easy fix.
- **Flows barely need it.** A flow's lookups bind their arguments from earlier results, preferring one the customer mentioned (see [the flow IR](../../crates/stretto-report/src/flow.rs)). Choosing which order holds "the lamp" is the one place a flow might use it, and that is not tested.

## Reproduce

[The CLI reference](../cli.md) lists every option.

Import the published answer bundles, then:

```sh
stretto match --tau2 ../tau2-bench --oracle replay --oracle-cache .oracle-cache --out match.md --json match.json
```

That reproduces [the report](matching-2026-09-24-report.md). The answers are in [this round's answer bundle](answers-2026-09-24-cold-manifest-match.md).
