# Flow search

Every flow so far acted on one threshold, 0.3, at every site, and had one decider. D0 weighs the habit with Jev's answers (the arbiter), and the habit-only flows use the habit alone. RFC-001 §3.10 plans a search over those settings with fugue-evo ([#25](https://github.com/alexnodeland/stretto/issues/25)). `stretto search` now runs it, and this round ran it in airline and retail.

- **What it searches.** A threshold for each site, from 0.10 to 0.95 in steps of 0.05, or the site switched off, and one decider for the flow: D0's arbiter or the habit alone. A site is the call the flow has just seen, such as `get_reservation_details`, or a `get_flight_status` that failed. Airline's D0 has 11 sites and retail's 19. A flow with its own thresholds is [flow format 2](../formats.md#thresholds).
- **How.** NSGA-II from [fugue-evo](https://github.com/alexnodeland/fugue-evo): one gene per site and one for the decider, simulated binary crossover and polynomial mutation. The first generation holds the three hand-set settings and random ones. Those settings are D0, the habit alone at 0.3, and arm C, the habit alone at 0.9. Airline ran 16 settings for 10 generations, and retail 12 for 8.
- **Two objectives, both from replays.** LLM turns saved, to raise, and detours, to lower, as `pilot/check_flow.py` counts them on GLM-5's recorded episodes. A front of the two answers for any price one would put on a detour, where a single score of turns saved less a price per detour would fix the price first. Every distinct setting is replayed, because [counterfactual evaluation](evaluate-2026-09-25.md) found that estimates from a flow's logs miss a changed decider's detours.
- **Not searched.** The caps on lookups, 8 per call and 40 per session, stayed as served, and so did the arbiter's fit. At D0 and the flows below, one airline call reached 8 lookups and no retail call did, and no episode in any replay made more than 17. At 0.1 everywhere, 5 airline calls and 32 retail calls reached 8, so a search that goes that low should search the per-call cap too.
- **Train, then test.** D0 was compiled from the four 2025 agents' episodes on τ²-bench's training tasks. The search replays GLM-5's episodes on the same tasks: 30 in airline and 74 in retail, four trials each. Then the hand-set settings and the front were replayed on GLM-5's test-task episodes, which the search never saw: 80 in airline and 160 in retail. The front is the settings no other replayed setting beat on both objectives.
- **A fair baseline.** A search has more knobs than a hand-set flow, so it is set against the best one knob does: each decider at one threshold everywhere, from 0.1 to 0.7, on the same episodes.
- **Answers.** The arbiter asks Jev at every decision. The search read Jev's answers from a cache and never asked. A decision whose question was not in the cache handed back, and each setting shows how many did (unanswered). Before the search, D0 was replayed on the training episodes at every threshold from 0.1 to 0.7, with Jev answering what the cache lacked. The replays on the test episodes asked Jev too, so they are exact.

## Findings

- **On GLM-5's test tasks, the search's front held flows with far fewer detours than D0,** for 7 more turns saved in airline and one fewer in retail.
  - Airline: F2 saved 48 LLM turns (7.6%) with 6 detours, where D0 saved 41 (6.5%) with 26. Over the 20 test tasks that is +7 turns (95% interval 0 to +15) and −20 detours (−49 to +2), resampling tasks.
  - Retail: F6 saved 293 turns (21.2%) with 30 detours, where D0 saved 294 with 58. Over the 40 test tasks, −1 turn (−3 to 0) and −28 detours (−68 to −2).
  - No single threshold did as well. The arbiter at 0.4 saved 35 airline turns with 15 detours, and 272 retail turns with 38.
- **Picked from the training front alone, the gains were smaller, most of all in retail.** F2 and F6 are the front's best on the test episodes, which a deployment would not have in hand. Take instead, on the training episodes, the setting with the fewest detours among those that saved at least D0's turns. In both domains that is F5: on the test episodes airline's saved 44 turns with 8 detours, and retail's 294 with 49. F6 looked like a trade of 28 turns on the training episodes, where 65 of its decisions went unanswered (below).
- **Each gain comes from one or two sites.** Replaying D0 with one site changed at a time, on the test episodes:
  - After `get_flight_status` in airline, F2's threshold of 0.60 kept D0's 41 turns and cut its detours from 26 to 11. No lookup there cleared 0.60, where D0 made 23.
  - After `get_reservation_details` in airline, F2's 0.10 took D0 to 48 turns, with 28 detours. An agent reads a customer's reservations one after another, and at 0.10 the flow reads on: 143 lookups there, where D0 made 131. With both sites changed, 48 turns and 6 detours.
  - After `get_product_details` in retail, F6's 0.90 cut D0's detours from 58 to 30 for one turn. No lookup there cleared 0.90, where D0 made 67.
  - Every other site the search changed, 9 in airline and 6 in retail, changed nothing on the test episodes.
- **[Promotion](promotion-2026-09-25.md) had held back the two sites whose thresholds the search raised,** after a flight status and after a product read. The search also lowers a threshold, which promotion cannot, and that is where airline's extra turns came from. The four settings on airline's front that saved the most with the arbiter (F2–F5) all put the flight-status site at 0.6 or above and the reservation site at 0.2 or below.
- **Another seed found the same two airline sites.** A second airline search, with seed 26, replayed 141 settings. Its best arbiter setting put 0.60 after a flight status and 0.15 after a reservation read, and saved 44 test turns with 11 detours. The exact threshold is worth a few turns: D0 with 0.60 and 0.15 at those sites saved 45 test turns with 11 detours, and with 0.60 and 0.10, as in F2, 48 with 6.
- **On two other agents, airline's F2 carried and retail's F6 traded.** Replayed on Claude Sonnet 4.5's and Qwen3.5's test-task episodes, F2 saved more turns than D0 with fewer detours: 120 against 116 with 57 detours against 77 for Sonnet, and 262 against 237 with 17 against 33 for Qwen. In retail, the lookups after a product read that F6 drops saved those agents turns. It gave up 15 of D0's turns for Sonnet and 33 for Qwen, for 17 and 24 fewer detours, and still did better on both counts than the arbiter at 0.4 everywhere. So part of what a search finds on one agent's episodes is that agent's habit, as with [predicate refinement](refine-2026-09-25.md), and a deployment should search on the sessions of the agent it will serve.
- **What did not carry.**
  - Airline's front ends in settings that saved 11–12 test turns with 0–7 detours, fewer than the arbiter at 0.5 everywhere: 28 turns with none.
  - The habit alone gained little, as with promotion. In airline, its best setting cut its test detours from 50 to 45 at the same 55 turns. In retail, the habit alone at 0.3 stayed the setting that saved the most at D0's detours: 311 turns with 58.
- **A search that never asks can misjudge a setting.** On the training episodes F6 traded 28 turns for 75 fewer detours: 429 turns with 23 detours, against D0's 457 with 98. But 65 of its decisions there handed back for want of an answer, 23 of them after an order read, and it made one more lookup there than D0 did. On the test episodes, with Jev answering, it made 13 more, and gave up one turn. The questions the search could not answer were few, 234 distinct ones in retail and 44 in airline, because settings share most of their states. So a search should let the replay ask. `stretto search` now says so when a setting on its front has unanswered decisions.

## Reproduce

[The CLI reference](../cli.md) lists every option. With the pilot's environment and GLM-5's results from `scripts/fetch-leaderboard.sh`, import every answer bundle in `docs/results/` into a fresh cache, then compile D0 and search. For retail, pass `--population 12 --generations 8`.

```sh
cargo build --release -p stretto-report
S=target/release/stretto
for b in docs/results/*answers*.jsonl.gz; do
  gunzip -c $b | $S import-answers --oracle-cache .oracle-cache
done
D=airline
$S compile --tau2 ../tau2-bench --domain $D --oracle replay --questions v2 \
  --predicates data/predicates-v2.json --no-intent --out d0-$D.flow.json
R=.data/tau2-targets/glm-5_enabled_${D}_gpt-5.2_4trials.json
TRAIN=$(python -c 'import json, sys; print(*json.load(open(sys.argv[1]))["train"])' \
  ../tau2-bench/data/tau2/domains/$D/split_tasks.json)
$S search --flow d0-$D.flow.json --population 16 --generations 10 --seed 25 \
  --dir runs/search-$D --json search-$D.json -- \
  python pilot/check_flow.py --domain $D --trials 0 1 2 3 --results $R --task-ids $TRAIN \
  --oracle-cache .oracle-cache --flow-oracle replay --in-process --jobs 8 --tau2 ../tau2-bench
$S search --flow d0-$D.flow.json --rescore search-$D.json --dir runs/test-$D -- \
  python pilot/check_flow.py --domain $D --trials 0 1 2 3 --results $R \
  --oracle-cache .oracle-cache --flow-oracle replay --in-process --jobs 8 --tau2 ../tau2-bench
```

The first search replays the training-task episodes. The second, with `--rescore`, replays the hand-set settings and the front on the test-task episodes, since `check_flow.py` replays the test split unless given `--task-ids`. The one-threshold rows are `check_flow.py` with `--flow d0-$D.flow.json --flow-decider arbiter` or `habit` and `--flow-threshold`. The site-by-site rows replay D0 with those sites' thresholds set in its `thresholds` (flow format 2). The rows on other agents replay their results in place of GLM-5's, fetched with `scripts/fetch-leaderboard.sh claude-sonnet-4.5 qwen3.5`.

From a fresh cache with only the published bundles, D0 compiled again the same, apart from its timestamp. Airline's search replayed the same 153 settings to the same totals and found the same front. The hand-set settings and every front replay to the same totals on both sets of episodes, and so do the 16 replays on other agents. The search reads only what the cache holds, so it reproduces from answers that were in the cache before it ran: the warm-up and the one-threshold baseline on the training episodes. Nothing asked afterwards touched a training episode's questions.
