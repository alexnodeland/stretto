# Counterfactual evaluation, validated on replays

RFC-001 §3.7 plans to evaluate a changed flow on the decisions an earlier flow logged, before it runs. A flow acts deterministically: it takes the likeliest lookup once its probability, times its binding's, reaches the threshold. So its logs say what it believed, not what else it might have done, and inverse propensity weights have nothing to reweight ([#15](https://github.com/alexnodeland/stretto/issues/15)). This round adds the missing pieces and tests them where the truth is known: replays of recorded episodes.

- **Exploration** (`stretto-proxy --flow-explore EPSILON`, `serve --explore`, `flow-serve --explore`). With probability ε the flow takes a lookup other than its rule's choice. The alternatives are the lookups that bind, drawn in proportion to the decider's probabilities. A wrong lookup is a detour, so this is the cheap, reversible exploration §3.7 allows. The draws are seeded by the episode's content, without the call ids a host makes up afresh, so a replay draws the same each time.
- **The policy view.** Each decision then logs every lookup the site offered, with the decider's and the habit's probabilities and the binding's chance and arguments. It also logs the rule's own choice and the chance that the flow took what it took (its propensity).
- **Labels.** `pilot/check_flow.py --explore` writes each decision with every option's outcome, as the replays count them. A lookup is *used* if the agent makes it later (a recorded call not yet made), a *detour* if it never does, and it earns *turn credit*: one over the calls of the recorded turn it spares a call of.
- **`stretto evaluate`** estimates what another rule would have done on those decisions. A rule is the arbiter's or the habit's probabilities at a threshold. It gives four estimates per site and in total:
  - **direct**: from every option's label;
  - **IPS**, **self-normalized IPS** and **doubly robust**: from the taken option's label alone, as the log of a flow that acts has only that one.

  Each site, and the total, reports its effective sample size, and the weighted estimates are refused below 10. Handing back is worth nothing on every measure, so the weighted estimates weigh only the decisions where the rule looks something up. Unit tests check all four on synthetic logs whose truth is known.

## Findings

- **Close to the logged rule, the four estimates agree.** For D0 itself and for the arbiter at 0.5, IPS, SNIPS and doubly robust land within about one standard error of the direct estimate. So the weights are right: at ε = 0.1 in retail, D0's detours are 69 directly and 74 ± 7, 72 and 70 ± 7 weighted.
- **Used lookups carry over to any rule.** Estimates of the lookups a rule would make that the agent made later land close to that rule's own replay, in both domains and at both rates. Direct and SNIPS land within 3.5%, IPS and doubly robust within 7%. For example, D0 in retail gets 608 against 598, and the habit alone 590–605 against 591.
- **Detours do not.** Even at the logged decisions, the direct estimate differs from each rule's own replay, because exploring changes where the flow goes next.
  - At ε = 0.1, D0's detours come out at 69 against 58 in retail, and 34 against 26 in airline. At ε = 0.2 they come out at 106 against 58.
  - The habit alone makes 50 detours in airline, twice D0's 26. Every estimate from D0's logs puts it between 26 and 42, level with D0, so all four would pick the wrong rule.
- **The detours a rule makes are mostly in its own chains.** About two-thirds of a flow's lookups come right after its own previous lookup, not after the agent's call. In airline, after the agent's calls the habit alone and D0 each make 8–9 detours. After their own lookups the habit alone makes 41 and D0 18. A chain's decisions depend on the flow's earlier choices, so one flow's log reaches another's chain only where the two chose alike. No per-decision estimate sees what lies beyond. Shadow mode has the same blind spot, since a flow that makes no lookups decides no chains.
- **Turn credit is not turns saved.** Summed over a rule's own decisions, it overstates the replay's turns saved by up to 75% (the arbiter at 0.5 in airline: 49.0 against 28). A turn is saved only when every call in it was made for the agent, and GLM-5 makes several calls in a turn. Only a replay counts that.
- **Exploring costs what it tests.** At ε = 0.1, D0 saved 281 retail turns instead of 294, with 129 detours instead of 58. In airline it saved 36 instead of 41, with 88 detours instead of 26.

So the estimates can rank changes that keep a flow's chains, such as a stricter threshold on the same decider, on used lookups and roughly on detours. They cannot price a change that chains differently, such as another decider, and they cannot say how many turns a change saves. For those, replay the new flow (`pilot/check_flow.py --flow`) and then run it paired, as the pilots do. Live logs add a further gap: the agent never makes a lookup the flow already made, so a taken lookup's outcome is hidden, and the weighted estimates have nothing to weigh. That holds unless used lookups are measured another way.

## The replay

GLM-5's recorded test episodes, all four trials: 160 retail episodes (1,384 LLM turns) and 80 airline (630). The logging flow is D0, compiled goal free from the training tasks exactly as for [the arms](arms-2026-09-24.md), exploring at ε = 0.1 and 0.2 (seed 15). Four rules are estimated from its logs:

- D0 itself (the arbiter at 0.3);
- the arbiter at 0.5;
- the habit alone at 0.3;
- arm C (the habit at 0.9).

Each rule's truth is its own replay, logged with ε = 0: the replay's turns saved, and at its own decisions its lookups, used lookups, detours and turn credit. Those replays give the published numbers of [the arms](arms-2026-09-24.md): D0 saves 294 retail turns and the habit alone 311, and in airline 41 and 55.

In the tables, *Its replay* is the rule's own. *Direct*, *IPS*, *SNIPS* and *DR* are estimated from the exploring D0's decisions. IPS and doubly robust show one standard error. The effective sample size is out of the rule's lookups at the logged decisions. No rule's lookup ever lacked support: each had a positive chance of being taken.

## Reproduce

[The CLI reference](../cli.md) lists every option. Import every answer bundle in `docs/results/` into a fresh cache and fetch GLM-5's published results with `scripts/fetch-leaderboard.sh`. Then replay D0 exploring, and each rule on its own:

```sh
R=.data/tau2-targets/glm-5_enabled_retail_gpt-5.2_4trials.json
cd pilot
python check_flow.py --domain retail --results ../$R --trials 0 1 2 3 --oracle-cache ../.oracle-cache \
  --flow-oracle replay --in-process --jobs 4 --explore 0.1 --explore-seed 15 --out runs/explore10
python check_flow.py --domain retail --results ../$R --trials 0 1 2 3 --oracle-cache ../.oracle-cache \
  --flow-oracle replay --in-process --jobs 4 --flow-decider habit --explore 0 --out runs/habit
stretto evaluate --decisions runs/explore10/decisions.jsonl \
  --target D0=arbiter@0.3 --target arbiter@0.5=arbiter@0.5 --target habit@0.3=habit@0.3 --target C=habit@0.9
```

From a fresh cache with only the published bundles, both ε = 0.1 replays give the same decisions, line for line, and the same totals.
