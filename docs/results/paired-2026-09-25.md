# Paired live run, 2026-09-25: every test task, with and without a flow

The pilots ran ten tasks per domain, too few to bound a loss in the pass rate. This run takes every test task of τ²-bench's retail and airline domains: 40 retail tasks once, and 20 airline tasks twice. Each task runs without and with the flow, and each pair is compared with itself. The pilots' 20 pairs are reused as they were recorded, and the other 60 pairs are new.

- **Agent:** GLM-5.3 in Claude Code, on Z.ai's GLM Coding Plan endpoint, with τ²-bench's system prompt and no built-in tools.
- **Tools:** τ²-bench's, over MCP, behind `stretto-proxy`.
- **Customer:** GLM-5.3, from τ²-bench's user-simulator prompt.
- **Flow:** the pilots' D0, compiled goal free from the 2025 baselines. It takes the likeliest lookup when the tool's probability times the binding's agreement is at least 0.3, and asks Jev live.
- **Reward:** τ²-bench's database check, as in the pilots. Airline's own reward is the database check times the communication check, which needs no judge; the airline section gives both. Retail's also counts natural-language assertions, which need an LLM judge and are left out.
- **Intervals:** 95%, from a bootstrap over tasks (4,000 draws; a task's two airline trials are drawn together, and the pooled estimate draws tasks within each domain). The pass rates also get an exact McNemar test on the pairs that disagree.
- **Budget:** at most 90% of a Lite GLM Coding Plan's weekly allowance, and at most 85% of any five-hour window. The runner checked a ledger of the credits billed before starting each episode. 1,897 credits in all, for the 120 new episodes. Three episodes failed as they started and were run again. The ledger booked them at their estimate, 49 credits in all, to be safe.

## Findings

- **Turns and tokens.** Over the 80 pairs of both domains, the flow cut the agent's LLM turns by 25.5% (95% interval 20.5% to 30.4%) and its input tokens by 21.0% (14.7% to 27.4%). Retail saved 30.4% of turns and airline 21.1%. 66 pairs took fewer turns with the flow, 8 the same and 6 more.
- **Pass rate.** 71 of 80 pairs passed without the flow and 70 with it: −1.25 points (−7.5 to +6.25; McNemar p = 1.0). Nine pairs disagree: five passed only without the flow and four only with it. By domain, retail lost 7.5 points (−17.5 to 0.0) and airline gained 5.0 (−5.0 to +17.5).
- **The gate.** RFC-001 set the gate for Phase 2 at 20% fewer LLM turns, with at most one point of pass^1 lost.
  - The turns half is met: the interval's lower end, 20.5%, clears it.
  - The other half is not shown, since the interval still allows a loss of 7.5 points. At this run's rate of disagreement, 9 pairs in 80, an interval of ±1 point would take about 4,300 pairs.
- **Why pairs disagree.** One of the nine plausibly comes from the flow: on retail task 79, its reads went deep on one order where the agent alone read wide. In the other eight, the failing step was the agent's or the simulated customer's, in a way no lookup touched:
  - a confirmation taken too early, and a cancellation reason chosen for the customer;
  - travel insurance the customer took in one arm and declined in the other, twice;
  - a bag fee charged after a downgrade;
  - the airline pilot's three.
- **Detours are rare and cheap.** 242 of the flow's 259 lookups were calls the agent also made in the pair's episode without the flow. The other 17 carried about 43,000 input tokens over the rest of their episodes. That is under 3% of the 1.56M input tokens the flow saved.

- **Turns.** Fewer turns in 37 of 40 pairs, the same in 2, and one more in 1: task 17, where the flow never acted. The 30 new pairs saved 32.4% of turns; the pilot's 10 saved 23.6%.
- **Passes.** Three pairs passed only without the flow, and none only with it. That is not significant (p = 0.25), but the interval allows a loss of up to 17.5 points, so retail alone cannot show that the pass rate holds. In each of the three, the flow was read-only and the failing write was the agent's:
  - *Task 79:* the flow read the customer's first order, then the bottle's product, and handed back. The agent went straight to the bottle's options and never opened the customer's other orders. So the simulated customer never learned that their other bottle was red, and asked for black. Without the flow, the agent read all three orders first. This one plausibly comes from the flow: its lookups went deep on one order where the agent alone went wide.
  - *Task 5:* the customer changed the plan (the lamp only) and restated it. The agent took that as the confirmation and made the exchange. Without the flow, it asked once more, and the customer's instructions turned that second confirmation into a return. No lookup was involved in the difference.
  - *Task 38:* the agent chose the cancellation reason itself ("ordered by mistake") instead of asking. Without the flow it asked, and the customer said "no longer needed". No lookup was involved.
- **Both failed:** tasks 12, 27 and 64. On 27 and 64 these are the pilot's episodes, with the same agent error in both arms.

## Airline: every test task, two trials

40 pairs: τ²-bench airline's 20 test tasks, two episodes in each arm. Ten of the first trial's pairs are the airline pilot's (marked *), and thirty are new.

| | Without the flow | With the flow | Difference (95% interval) |
|---|---|---|---|
| LLM turns | 535 | 422 | −21.1% (−12.3% to −30.0%); −2.8 per episode (−1.4 to −4.7) |
| Agent input tokens | 4.17M | 3.42M | −18.0% (−7.5% to −28.4%) |
| Z.ai credits | 755.7 | 673.0 | −10.9% |
| Agent's own calls | 335 | 233 | the flow made 114 lookups; the agent repeated 2 |
| Passed (database check) | 34 | 36 | +5.0 points (−5.0 to +17.5); McNemar p = 0.69 |
| Passed (airline's reward: database × communication) | 34 | 36 | +5.0 points (−5.0 to +17.5); the same episodes pass |

- **Turns.** Fewer turns in 29 of 40 pairs, the same in 6, and more in 5. The flow made lookups in 37 of its 40 episodes. The 30 new pairs saved 22.3% of turns; the pilot's 10 saved 17.3%.
- **Passes.** Four pairs passed only with the flow and two only without it (p = 0.69).
  - *With the flow only.* Tasks 6 and 18, trial 1, are the pilot's pairs. Without the flow, the agent booked a flight on task 6, which expects no write, and added a paid bag nobody asked for on task 18 ([the pilot](pilot-airline-2026-09-24.md)). The other two are task 29, in both trials. The customer's plan needs a new booking, and the agent asked whether to add travel insurance, which the customer's instructions do not mention. The simulated customer said yes in both episodes without the flow and no in both with it. The task expects none. The flow's one lookup there, the reservation, was the agent's own.
  - *Without the flow only.* Task 31, trial 1, is the pilot's pair. With the flow, the agent upgraded and changed the flights at a net price the task counts against the customer's limit ([the pilot](pilot-airline-2026-09-24.md)). The other is task 18, trial 2. After downgrading all five reservations to economy, the agent charged a $50 fee for a third checked bag that economy's allowance no longer covered. The task expects no change to the bags, and the pilot's episode without the flow failed the same way. The flow's six reservation reads were ones the agent also made without it.
  - *Both failed:* task 44, in both trials. In all four episodes the agent upgraded two of the three reservations the task expects, and left out the third (KC18K6).
- **Airline's own reward.** Rescored with the communication check as well, every episode keeps the database check's verdict (`rescore.py --scoring basis`). That includes task 18, the one airline test task with a figure the agent must tell the customer.

## Cost

**Z.ai credits.** Retail cost 565.2 credits without the flow and 473.1 with it (−16.3%). Airline cost 755.7 and 673.0 (−10.9%). Across both domains that is 1,320.9 and 1,146.1, 13.2% less. Credits fall less than turns, partly because they also count the simulated customer, whom the flow does not change. Jev's answers are billed separately and are not in these credits.

**Detours.** A lookup is the agent's own when the agent made the same call, with the same arguments, in the pair's episode without the flow. Otherwise it is a detour. A detour costs its result's tokens as they enter the context. It then costs them again at every later turn, which reads the context anew; this is the carried cost below.

| | Retail | Airline |
|---|---|---|
| The flow's lookups | 145 | 114 |
| The agent's own | 140 | 102 |
| Detours | 5, in 3 pairs | 12, in 6 pairs |
| Detour results, in tokens | 4,389 (median 936) | 1,233 (median 2) |
| Carried over the rest of their episodes | 23,618 | 19,219 |
| Input tokens the flow saved | 808,848 | 752,277 |

- Retail's detours were all product reads (`get_product_details`), 700 to 1,000 tokens each.
- Airline's were eight flight statuses (`get_flight_status`), a word each, and four reservations of about 300 tokens.
- The agent repeated 6 of the flow's lookups: 4 in retail and 2 in airline.
- Tokens are counted with tiktoken's `cl100k_base`, a stand-in for GLM's tokenizer. The turns after each lookup come from Claude Code's event stream.

## The episodes

All 160 episodes, the pilots' 40 included, are in [paired-2026-09-25-episodes.tar.gz](paired-2026-09-25-episodes.tar.gz) (1.8 MB). They are laid out as the runner wrote them:

- `retail/{baseline,flows}/task-<id>/`;
- `airline/{baseline,flows}/task-<id>/`, with the second airline trial under `airline/trial-1/`.

Each episode holds the files [the episodes page](episodes-2026-09-24.md#what-each-episode-holds) lists. As there, Claude Code's event streams are left out.

[`paired-2026-09-25.json`](paired-2026-09-25.json) holds:

- every pair's numbers;
- each domain's summary, and the pooled one;
- every lookup the flow made, with whether it was the agent's own, its tokens, and the turns after it.

## Reproduce

[`pilot/run_paired.py`](../../pilot/run_paired.py) ran the episodes, and [`pilot/analyze_paired.py`](../../pilot/analyze_paired.py) reports on them. Run both in τ²-bench's Python environment ([`pilot/README.md`](../../pilot/README.md)).

**From the archive.** Rescore every episode, then compute each domain's pairs and the pooled estimate:

```sh
mkdir -p /tmp/episodes && tar -xzf docs/results/paired-2026-09-25-episodes.tar.gz -C /tmp/episodes
E=/tmp/episodes/paired-2026-09-25-episodes
cd pilot
python rescore.py $E/retail/baseline $E/retail/flows $E/airline/baseline $E/airline/flows \
  $E/airline/trial-1/baseline $E/airline/trial-1/flows
# 160 episodes rescored; 0 differ from the recorded reward
python rescore.py --scoring basis --json /tmp/airline-basis.json $E/airline/baseline $E/airline/flows \
  $E/airline/trial-1/baseline $E/airline/trial-1/flows
python analyze_paired.py pairs $E/retail retail 1
python analyze_paired.py pairs $E/airline airline 2 --basis /tmp/airline-basis.json
python analyze_paired.py pool $E/retail $E/airline
```

`analyze_paired.py detours` counts the turns after each lookup from Claude Code's event streams, which the archive leaves out. Its rows are in `paired-2026-09-25.json`.

**Run it again.** This needs `ZAI_API_KEY`, and `TYPESAFE_API_KEY` for the flows arm. Pass the pilots' archives as `--reuse` to keep their pairs:

```sh
python run_paired.py retail --oracle-cache ../.oracle-cache --reuse /tmp/episodes/pilot-2026-09-24-episodes
python run_paired.py airline --trials 2 --oracle-cache ../.oracle-cache \
  --reuse /tmp/episodes/pilot-airline-2026-09-24-episodes
```

Before each episode, the runner checks the credits it has billed (`--ledger`). It waits at `--window-cap` and stops at `--week-cap`.
