# Confirmation, judged by a System-One model

τ²-bench's policies ask the agent to describe a change and get the customer's explicit yes before it writes. The guards check this with a word list: does the customer's last message contain a confirming word? The [guard audit](guards-2026-09-24.md) found that the list misses about as often in successful episodes as in failed ones, so `retail.confirmed` and `airline.confirmed` are logged, not enforced. RFC-001 names judging a confirmation as a System-One question. This run puts that question to Jev.

## Method

`stretto confirm` asks jev-1.13.0 one yes/no question per write in τ²-bench's published trajectories: the four 2025 agents, all tasks, four trials each. Each question shows Jev:

- what the agent said last before the customer's last message;
- that message;
- the call about to be made: the tool and its arguments.

The question: did the customer's reply explicitly agree to this change? Jev fails a write when its probability of a yes is below 0.5. There were 3,863 questions, and they cost $0.12. [The full report](confirm-2026-09-24-report.md) has the examples.

## Against episode outcomes

As in the guard audit, a failure on an accepted write of a successful episode is either a false alarm or a skipped confirmation that the database check did not catch.

| | Retail: word list fails | Retail: Jev fails | Airline: word list fails | Airline: Jev fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Accepted writes, successful episodes | 61 of 1,914 (3.2%) | 95 (5.0%) | 21 of 303 (6.9%) | 33 (10.9%) |
| Accepted writes, failed episodes | 28 of 726 (3.9%) | 62 (8.5%) | 35 of 541 (6.5%) | 45 (8.3%) |
| Episodes with such a write: successful | 39 of 1,324 (2.9%) | 67 (5.1%) | 9 of 431 (2.1%) | 13 (3.0%) |
| Episodes with such a write: failed | 20 of 500 (4.0%) | 42 (8.4%) | 19 of 369 (5.1%) | 25 (6.8%) |

Jev flags more writes than the word list does. In retail it separates failed episodes from successful ones better, 8.5% against 5.0% of accepted writes. In airline it does not, at the level of single writes. Episode outcome is a weak test, though. τ²-bench checks the database, not whether the customer agreed.

## Against hand labels

On 94.9% of the 3,484 accepted writes, the two judges agree. On the other 178, one says confirmed and the other does not. A random 40 of them were drawn, 20 of each kind, shuffled, and labelled blind to both judges. The label was "confirmed" only when the agent had described this exact change and the customer's last message agreed to it.

| Where they disagree | Writes | Jev right | Word list right |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jev says no, the word list says yes | 20 | 16 | 4 |
| Jev says yes, the word list says no | 20 | 14 | 6 |
| All | 40 | **30 (75%)** | 10 (25%) |

Weighted by how often each kind of disagreement occurs, Jev is right on 78% of them. One annotator labelled them, and that annotator is Claude, the model that ran this analysis. The labels, and exactly what was shown for each, are in [confirm-2026-09-24-labels.json](confirm-2026-09-24-labels.json).

What the word list misses:

- **A "yes" to something else.** "Yes, please process those returns," and the agent cancels an order. "Yes, please use gift_card_8190333," and the agent charges a credit card. "Sure, my user ID is sophia_silva_7557," and the agent cancels the reservation.
- **A "yes" with the request withdrawn.** "Actually, before you proceed, I've changed my mind."
- **Agreement without the words.** "Please book the second option," or "then please revert us both back to economy class."

Where Jev is wrong:

- **It says yes too easily (6 of the 10).** In these cases the customer asked for a change the agent never described first. Examples are "Please update it for all orders" before any address was read back, and "Please cancel both reservations" before either was described.
- **It says no too easily (4).** Here the customer agrees but changes a detail or adds a question. Examples are "Yes, please proceed. But I'd prefer any extra payment on a gift card," and "Yeah, that's fine. So it's $22.55 extra, right?"

The first kind could be fixed by a second question: did the agent describe this exact change before the reply?

## What this means for the guards

- **Jev is the better judge of a confirmation**, by a wide margin where the two disagree. The word list stays logged only.
- **The mismatches are the valuable catch.** The customer agrees to one change, and the agent makes another: a refund to the wrong card, a cancellation instead of a return. No word list can see that, because it never looks at the call.
- **Enforcing Jev's judgment would refuse 5–11% of accepted writes in successful episodes.** The word list fails about half of those too. Of the rest, 16 of the 20 labelled were real lapses. Whether refusing them costs pass^1 or turns (the agent has to ask again) is for a live test to show. The guards do not enforce it yet.
