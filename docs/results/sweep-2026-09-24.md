# Fewer traces: where the System-One model carries the savings

The arms replays found that a read-only flow saves as many turns on its habit alone as with D0's arbiter, which weighs the habit against Jev's answers and the predicates ([arms](arms-2026-09-24.md)). That habit learned from 831 successful retail episodes and 246 airline ones: four agents on all of τ²-bench's training tasks. A new deployment starts with far fewer. RFC-001 asks where a System-One model earns its place (§6, question 9), and one answer it proposed is traces: with fewer, the habit is weaker, and a zero-shot decider may carry the savings. This sweep trains the habit on less.

## Method

- **Fewer tasks.** `--train-fraction F`, on `stretto phase0` and `stretto compile`, keeps a fixed share of the training tasks, at least one. The share was meant to be pseudo-random, but it is clustered (see the correction below). The order does not depend on the share, so every smaller sample is part of every larger one. Retail was cut to 1, 3, 7, 19 and 37 of its 74 training tasks, airline to 1, 3, 8 and 15 of its 30.
- **What shrinks.** The habit, the sites a flow acts at, and the argument bindings learn from the successful episodes of those tasks alone.
- **What does not.** The arbiter is fitted as always, on Jev's answers at the source agents' held-out decisions, cross-fitted over task folds. The habit's prediction is one of its inputs, so each size gets its own arbiter. A site's lookups are part of its questions, so Jev was asked again wherever a smaller sample changed them. At every size the arbiter's eight weights were fitted on at least 2,344 held-out retail decisions and 992 airline ones. A deployment with few traces would have far fewer, so this sweep flatters the arbiter.
- **The replay.** Each flow replays GLM-5's recorded test episodes, all four trials, through [`pilot/check_flow.py`](../../pilot/check_flow.py), as in the arms results: 160 retail episodes (1,384 LLM turns) and 80 airline (630). A recorded turn whose calls the flow had already made is a turn saved; a flow lookup the agent never made is a detour. Both deciders take the likeliest lookup at 0.3. Neither ever saw these tasks. The flows compiled from every task reproduce the arms results exactly.

## Correction: the samples are clustered, not random

`--train-fraction` ranked tasks by an FNV-1a hash of `train-sample/<task id>`, and kept the first ones. FNV-1a barely mixes the last characters of a string. So ids that share a prefix sort next to each other, and the order runs roughly from the highest task ids down:

| Share | Retail tasks | Airline tasks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 task | 109 | 49 |
| 3 tasks | 104, 105, 109 | 42, 43, 49 |
| 7–8 tasks | 103–107, 109, 113 | 14, 40–43, 46, 47, 49 |

Neighbouring τ²-bench tasks often share a customer and a kind of request. So the small samples here are narrower than a random draw of the same size would be.

The measurements stand for the tasks that were drawn. But where the crossover lies, and how the habit does on few traces, rest on one clustered draw, and the order is not the "fixed pseudo-random share" the method describes.

`stretto` now mixes the hash before ranking (splitmix64's finalizer), so `--train-fraction` draws different, spread-out samples. The samples used here are listed in [sweep-2026-09-24-samples.json](sweep-2026-09-24-samples.json). `--train-tasks` names a sample exactly, so the current stretto rebuilds each flow of this page byte for byte, apart from its compile time. [The cold start](cold-start-2026-09-24.md) asks the same question on random samples of an agent's own sessions.

Four findings:

1. **With enough traces, the habit carries the savings.** From 7 retail tasks (67 successful episodes) and 8 airline tasks (75), the habit alone saves as much as it does with every task, and 1–3 points more than the arbiter. What the arbiter buys there is precision: fewer detours at every size in airline, and at every size but the full one in retail.
2. **Below that, Jev's answers carry them.**
   - With 3 retail tasks (21 episodes), the arbiter saves 20.4% of turns, almost all it saves with every task. The habit alone saves 1.8%. It is unsure where agents read the next order: its probability for that is 0.19 at the median and never above 0.25. So under the 0.3 rule it never reads an order, where the arbiter reads 394.
   - With a single retail task (3 episodes), the habit copies that task's path. It reads product lists nobody asked for, makes 260 detours, and saves 4.9%. The arbiter saves 10.0% with 28 detours.
   - With 3 airline tasks, the arbiter saves 5.6% with 1 detour, and the habit 4.1% with 37. The difference is not resolved (−3.3 to +0.2).
3. **The traces still set a flow's reach.** A flow acts only at sites where training shows a lookup, and offers only the lookups it saw there. Retail's flow grows from 4 sites and 6 lookups at one task to 19 and 44 with every task. Airline's knows 2 sites up to 8 tasks, 6 at 15 and 11 at 30. With one airline task, the arbiter made 11 lookups, all of them the agent's own, and neither decider saved a turn. Jev chooses among what the traces offer; it cannot add to it.
4. **Each size is one draw of tasks, and a clustered one** (see the correction). At 1 and 3 tasks, which tasks are drawn matters: the habit did worse on three tasks than on one. So the sweep locates a crossover for these tasks, between 3 and 7 retail tasks, rather than tracing a curve.

## What this means

- **A System-One model earns its place in read-only flows when traces are scarce.** A flow compiled from a handful of sessions should keep the arbiter. As traces accumulate, the habit takes over the savings, and Jev's answers buy precision. *Amended:* not for a deployment's own first sessions. From five random sessions of GLM-5's own, the habit alone saved more than an arbiter fitted on those sessions ([the cold start](cold-start-2026-09-24.md)). This finding holds for the clustered samples here, scored by an arbiter fitted on thousands of other agents' decisions.
- **The caveat is the arbiter's own data.** Here it was fitted on thousands of held-out decisions at every size. `stretto learn` fits it on 30% of a deployment's own sessions, so with few sessions it has few decisions, and how well eight weights fit from those is untested. *Tested in [the cold start](cold-start-2026-09-24.md):* fitted on 3 to 11 decisions, the weights land anywhere, and the flow trails the habit alone until about twenty sessions.
- **Jev does not widen a flow's reach.** Sites and options come from traces. A flow whose options also came from the tool manifest, with Jev choosing among every read-only tool, would reach further on few traces. It is not built. *Built and tested in [options from the manifest](manifest-options-2026-09-24.md):* it reaches no further that saves turns, because a lookup no trace showed has no learned bindings.

## Cost

No LLM ran. Jev answered 10,681 new questions for the sweep, 26.7M input tokens, $1.12. They are published in [this round's answer bundle](answers-2026-09-24-arms-sweep-confirm.md).

## Reproduce

[The CLI reference](../cli.md) lists every option.

Import the three answer bundles as [the bundle's page](answers-2026-09-24-arms-sweep-confirm.md) shows. Then compile each size's flow from the cache, naming its sample, and replay it with each decider:

```sh
for f in 0.0135 0.04 0.1 0.25 0.5; do
  tasks=$(jq -r ".retail[\"$f\"] | join(\",\")" docs/results/sweep-2026-09-24-samples.json)
  stretto compile --tau2 ../tau2-bench --domain retail --oracle replay --oracle-cache .oracle-cache \
    --questions v2 --predicates data/predicates-v2.json --train-tasks $tasks --out flows/retail-$f.flow.json
done
cd pilot
for decider in arbiter habit; do
  python check_flow.py --domain retail --flow ../flows/retail-0.04.flow.json --trials 0 1 2 3 \
    --results ../.data/tau2-targets/glm-5_enabled_retail_gpt-5.2_4trials.json \
    --oracle-cache ../.oracle-cache --flow-oracle replay --flow-decider $decider --flow-threshold 0.3 \
    --out runs/sweep/retail-0.04-$decider
done
```

The retail shares 0.0135, 0.04, 0.1, 0.25 and 0.5 are 1, 3, 7, 19 and 37 tasks; the full flow takes no `--train-tasks`. For airline, use `--domain airline`, GLM-5's airline results and the shares 0.034, 0.1, 0.25 and 0.5 (1, 3, 8 and 15 tasks). Stretto at `55ff505` gives the same samples from `--train-fraction`. Every flow recompiles from the published answers to the one replayed here, and every replay reads only published answers. `stretto compile` now warns when a question goes unanswered, since the arbiter is then fitted without it: the first compile of the 7-task retail flow missed one answer to a network error, and the flow replayed here is the recompiled one.

Per-episode rows for every replay, and the comparison above, are in [sweep-2026-09-24.json](sweep-2026-09-24.json).
