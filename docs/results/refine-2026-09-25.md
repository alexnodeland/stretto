# Predicate refinement, in airline

The arbiter weighs three predicates, yes/no questions about the state that were written by hand (`data/predicates-v2.json`): `list_pending`, `needs_options` and `must_ask`. RFC-001 §3.4 plans to find predicates rather than write them ([#16](https://github.com/alexnodeland/stretto/issues/16)). Airline is where the current ones do worst: the audit found the airline flow picking the agent's own step 64.5% of the time, against 85.9% in retail ([the audit](audit-2026-09-24.md)). This round builds the loop and runs it there once.

- **Find the weakest sites.** `stretto refine --examples` fits the arbiter on Phase 0b v2's decision log, by cross-validation over tasks as Phase 0b does, and ranks the sites by the held-out surprise of the agents' steps. At the five worst, it writes out the decisions where the arbiter put the least probability on the agent's step, one per task, each with the state the System-One model saw ([the examples](refine-2026-09-25-examples.md)).
- **Propose.** Whoever reads the examples writes candidate predicates. Here that was Claude, the model running the analysis. It proposed eight ([the candidates](refine-2026-09-25-candidates.json)).
- **Answer.** `phase0 --candidates` asks each candidate alone at every asked next-step decision, with the same state as the next-step question. So no other answer, and no other cache key, changes.
- **Keep what explains the data.** `stretto refine` refits the arbiter with each candidate added. It keeps candidates greedily while one raises the held-out log-likelihood of the agents' steps by more than 3.8 nats, which is what BIC charges a parameter at 2,028 decisions.
- **Check out of sample.** The proposer saw examples from 17 of the 20 test tasks, so cross-validation over tasks cannot rule out that it fitted what it saw. GLM-5's published airline test episodes serve as the check. The same arbiters judge its 556 decisions, but none of them is fitted on, and the proposer saw none.
- **Data:** the four 2025 baselines' held-out airline decisions, 2,042 of them, from the goal-free v2 questions. Before this round, those questions reproduced the published numbers exactly: 12.6% of turns saved with lookup first at p ≥ 0.3 ([goal free](phase0b-v2-goal-free-2026-09-24-summary.md)).

Many of the misses pointed at one named lookup, such as the customer's profile, a fare search or a flight's status. The existing shapes cannot say that: a predicate bore on the same lookup again, on every lookup, or on handing back. So a predicate can now bear on one named lookup, `"favors": {"lookup": TOOL}` ([formats](../formats.md)). Five of the eight candidates use it.

## Findings

- **Two of the eight candidates raised the held-out likelihood.**
  - `answer_in_hand`, "do the results so far already hold what the customer's latest message asks for?", bears on handing back. Alone it gained 45.4 nats.
  - `onestop_needed`, "has a direct-flight search come back without a flight that suits the request?", bears on the one-stop search. It gained 15.3 nats more.

  Together they took the surprise from 0.654 to 0.624 nats per decision, and agreement from 74.8% to 76.7%.
- **Five made the fit worse, and one gained less than the penalty.** The worst were `needs_status` (−27.7 nats) and `needs_profile` (−16.9), each bearing on a named lookup. A weight fitted on four folds did worse on the fifth, so what they tracked did not hold from one set of tasks to the next.
- **Neither kept predicate carried to GLM-5.** At GLM-5's 556 decisions, which the proposer never saw, the pair lowered the log-likelihood by 1.5 nats (−398.3 to −399.8), and agreement moved from 72.7% to 73.0%. `answer_in_hand` alone gained 0.8 nats there, and `onestop_needed` lost 1.2.
- **Nor did they move the savings.** With lookup first at p ≥ 0.3, the flows' rule, pooled over the four source agents: 12.6% of turns saved before and 12.5% after, with detours up from 102.8 to 106.6 per 100 episodes. For GLM-5, savings fell from 7.1% to 6.5%, and episodes with a detour rose from 56.2% to 61.3%. At the stricter combined p ≥ 0.5, detours fell from 62.5 to 57.8 per 100 episodes at the same savings (11.0% and 10.9%).

So one round of refinement found predicates that fit the agents whose decisions the proposer read, and nothing more. Two explanations fit this, and one round cannot tell them apart:

- the proposer fitted what it saw, since its examples came from most of the tasks;
- what the kept predicates track, such as when an agent reports back rather than looking further, is each agent's own habit, not a fact about the state that sets every agent's next step.

The three hand-written predicates stay. `refine` stays as the check. A kept candidate should raise the likelihood of an agent the proposer never saw, not only on tasks the arbiter was not fitted on.

**Some misses turn on what the state keeps only in part.** In one example, the agent had read all six of the customer's reservations and moved on to searching fares, where the arbiter expected another reservation read. `reservation_unread` answered yes there (0.78). By then the profile that lists the six was a single line in the state, as every lookup older than the latest four is. So whether any reservation was left could be told only by counting the reads against the agent's own message.

## Reproduce

[The CLI reference](../cli.md) lists every option. Import every answer bundle in `docs/results/`, and fetch GLM-5's published results with `scripts/fetch-leaderboard.sh`:

```sh
for b in docs/results/*answers*.jsonl.gz; do
  gunzip -c $b | stretto import-answers --oracle-cache .oracle-cache
done
C=docs/results/refine-2026-09-25-candidates.json
R=.data/tau2-targets/glm-5_enabled_airline_gpt-5.2_4trials.json
stretto phase0 --tau2 ../tau2-bench --domain airline --target glm-5=$R --oracle replay --questions v2 \
  --predicates data/predicates-v2.json --no-intent --candidates $C \
  --oracle-log log.jsonl --oracle-dump dump.jsonl
stretto refine --log log-airline.jsonl --candidates $C --examples examples.md --dump dump-airline.jsonl
```

`refine` gives the tables above exactly. The examples come out as published, with each example also listing the candidates' answers. The savings with the kept pair come from the same `phase0` run with `--weigh answer_in_hand --weigh onestop_needed`.
