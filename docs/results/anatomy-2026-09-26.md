# What decides each step: the anatomy of τ²-bench episodes

How much of an agent's work could a workflow compiled once from its traces do without a model? A compiled step can run on its own only if everything it needs is in the traces: which tool comes next, and each argument's value. This page classes every LLM turn, every decision after a tool returns and every argument of every call by where that information came from, on ten agents' retail and airline episodes. No such count had been published: [the literature review](../research/compiling-workflows-2026-09-26.md) found none, and TraceCompiler, the nearest system, reports no compile-or-decline rate.

## Findings

- **Replies and the calls that answer the customer are 60–75% of LLM turns.** Replies to the customer are 30–53%, and calls made right after the customer speaks another 18–29%. Their content is the customer's words, which no trace fixes in advance.
- **What traces can fix is the read skeleton: 19–32% of retail turns and 10–31% of airline turns.** These are reads after a tool returns whose every argument sits alone at its path in an earlier result (10–13% of retail turns, but 1–4% of airline), or that walk every record of a list the agent read (9–23% in retail, 9–28% in airline). That is the work stretto's read-only flow already does: live, it saved 25.5% of turns.
- **Picking one record of several needs the customer.** Reads that pick some of a list's records, and not all, are another 3–8% of turns, and reads that need a value the customer gave or the agent made up 1–10%.
- **Write arguments are copied, but chosen.** 95% of retail and 92% of airline write arguments appear in an earlier tool result. In airline, 38% of them are one of several values at their path (which flight, which passenger), and 16% were picked from a list the agent did not use in full. The customer's own words name 36% of write arguments in both domains.
- **After a tool returns, the previous tool alone predicts the next step 50–84% of the time.** The best single structured feature of the results, such as a status or whether listed records are still unread, adds up to 11 points and nothing for the most habitual agents. Knowing the episode's goal, in hindsight, adds at most 4 more.
- **Rules a compiled workflow could run alone are few, and not write-grade.** Rules that were at least 95% sure on at least ten training cases cover 2–44% of retail decisions (under 9% for seven of ten agents) and 0–16% of airline decisions, and hold 84–100% on held-out tasks, not the 99% a write needs.
- **The habit belongs to the agent.** GLM-5 and Claude Opus 4.5 are habitual: such rules cover 36–44% of their retail decisions. Claude Sonnet 4.5 and GPT-5.2 are not: 2–4%. A workflow compiled from one agent's traces reaches as far as that agent's habits.

So compiling once works for the part stretto already compiles, and the rest of an episode is language: what the customer asks, which record they mean, and what to say back. More of it can be taken over by a cheaper model reading the conversation, not by more compilation.

Telecom, where the agent troubleshoots by a procedure over tool results, is the other case: there a tree over the whole state predicts more than one feature, and in τ²'s solo mode the procedure is most of the episode ([where compiling once works](telecom-anatomy-2026-09-26.md)).

## Method

[`scripts/anatomy.py`](../../scripts/anatomy.py) reads τ²-bench results files, or folders of episodes' `simulation.json`. The rows are the nine current leaderboard agents' published trajectories (four trials of every task: 456 retail and 200 airline episodes each) and the baseline arm of [the paired run](paired-2026-09-25.md) (GLM-5.3 in Claude Code, 40 episodes per domain, so its rows are noisy).

**Turns.** Every assistant message after the greeting is a turn: a reply, or calls. A turn with a write (or a hand-off) counts as a write. A turn of reads is classed by what follows it (the customer or a tool) and, after a tool, by the neediest of its calls:

- **bound:** every argument is copied, one value at its path;
- **every record:** it copies values from a list the agent went on to use in full;
- **picked:** it copies from a list the agent used only in part;
- **customer or made up:** some argument appears in no earlier result.

**Arguments** follow TraceCompiler's binding classes ([arXiv 2608.02680](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02680)). *Copied* means the value appears verbatim in an earlier tool result. It is *one value at its path* when some result holds it at a path with no other values there (a user id, an order's own id), and *one of several* when it only ever sits in a list, among other values at the same path (a user's orders, an order's items). *The customer's* values appear only in what the customer wrote; *made up* values appear nowhere earlier (closed-set choices, sums, dates, summaries), unless every call of the tool gives the argument one value, which makes it a constant (none were). A copied value the customer also wrote is counted as named by the customer.

**Decisions** after a tool returns (the next call's tool, or a reply) are predicted three ways, each fitted on half the tasks and scored on the other half, both ways:

- **sequence:** the commonest next step after that tool and its outcome;
- **+ structure:** the one structured feature of the results so far that best predicts it at that tool, chosen by cross-validation within the training half. Features are a result's short field values (a status, a cabin), list lengths (none, one, several), and whether records listed earlier are still unread;
- **+ goal:** the same, with the episode's goal as a feature: the set of writes it makes, known only in hindsight.

A rule is *sure* when its prediction held in at least 95% of at least ten training cases.

## Caveats

- Copies are matched verbatim. A value the agent reformatted, or took from a result as part of a longer string, counts as made up; a short value that happens to occur in a result counts as copied. Numbers of fewer than three digits and strings of fewer than three characters are never matched to the customer's words.
- "Every record" assumes that reading a whole list is the workflow. An agent that reads every order because the customer's item was in the last one is counted the same as one that reads every order by habit.
- The goal is known in hindsight, from the episode's writes. A workflow would have to learn it from the customer, so the "+ goal" column is an upper bound on what naming the goal can add.
- The rows describe agents in τ²-bench's harness, whose calling styles differ (the share of turns in each class moves with how often an agent batches calls), and GLM-5.3's are one harness and 40 episodes.

## Reproduce

No keys. With the published trajectories fetched:

```sh
python3 scripts/anatomy.py $(scripts/fetch-leaderboard.sh all | cut -d= -f2) --json anatomy.json
```

And the paired run's baseline arm, from [its episodes](paired-2026-09-25-episodes.tar.gz):

```sh
python3 scripts/anatomy.py --domain retail paired-2026-09-25-episodes/retail/baseline
python3 scripts/anatomy.py --domain airline paired-2026-09-25-episodes/airline/baseline paired-2026-09-25-episodes/airline/trial-1/baseline
```

[anatomy-2026-09-26.json](anatomy-2026-09-26.json) holds every number above, with each agent's accuracy at its commonest sites.
